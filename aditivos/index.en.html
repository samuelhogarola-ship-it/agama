What an additive for plastics is and what it does

An additive is used to modify or improve a specific behavior of the material or process: mold release, moisture control, slip, protection, stability, cleaning, appearance, or part performance.

The choice should start from the real problem. Before quoting, it helps to define what is happening on the shop floor, what resin is used, how it is processed, and what result you want to measure.

What problem do you need to solve?

  • Improve release or mold release.
  • Control moisture during processing.
  • Support stability or protection.
  • Optimize appearance or performance.

Main needs that additives address

Not all additives solve the same problem. Separating the need avoids requesting the wrong item and helps design a useful trial.

What we review before recommending an additive

Resin

We confirm the base material and whether there are fillers, recycled content, pigments, or other components.

Process

The recommendation changes between injection molding, extrusion, blow molding, and temperature conditions.

Expected result

There must be a measurable goal: better release, drying, protection, cleaning, slip, or stabilization.

For a faster quote

Share your resin, process, observed problem, end application, working temperature, current dosage if any, and estimated volume. With that data AGAMA can better assess compatibility before moving to pricing.

Frequently asked questions about additives for plastics

What should I review before choosing an additive?

Define the processing issue, resin, temperature, dosage, finish, application, and measurable result.

What types of needs can AGAMA help with?

Processing, mold release, moisture, protection, stability, appearance, and performance depending on the application.

What information do I need to request a quote?

Share:

  • Resin.
  • Process.
  • Observed problem.
  • End application.
  • Temperature or critical condition.
  • Estimated quantity.

Does the catalog replace a technical review?

No. The catalog helps review options; the final selection should be validated by resin, process, and a real-conditions trial.

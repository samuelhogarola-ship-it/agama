White Masterbatch MB-120 Blanco Shalom: Titanium Dioxide Opacity for Plastics

AGAMA's Blanco Shalom MB-120 is a titanium dioxide-based white masterbatch that delivers high opacity and bright, consistent white in polyolefin applications. Here is what it offers, where it fits, and how to source it.

What MB-120 Blanco Shalom is

MB-120 is a concentrated white masterbatch built on a polyethylene carrier resin with titanium dioxide (TiO2) as the primary pigment. TiO2 is the industry standard for white opacity in plastics — it scatters light more effectively than any other white pigment, producing a bright, clean white even at low loading levels.

AGAMA formulates MB-120 in-house under the trade name Blanco Shalom. The formulation targets a balance between high TiO2 loading and processability, ensuring smooth dispersion without die buildup or plate-out during extrusion or injection molding.

Key specifications

  • Pigment: Titanium dioxide (rutile grade)
  • Carrier resin: Polyethylene (LDPE/LLDPE compatible)
  • Recommended let-down: 2% to 6% by weight
  • Compatible processes: Injection molding, blown film, cast film, extrusion, blow molding
  • Compatible resins: PE, PP, and most polyolefins
  • Reference price: $68 MXN per kilogram

Applications

White masterbatch is one of the highest-volume color concentrates in the plastics industry. MB-120 is used across a wide range of applications:

  • Packaging films: Bread bags, retail bags, shrink wrap — anywhere a bright white background is needed for print registration
  • Rigid packaging: Caps, closures, bottles, and containers where the white needs to be opaque against the contents
  • Household goods: Buckets, storage containers, laundry baskets, and kitchen products
  • Agricultural film: White/black mulch films where the white side reflects sunlight to regulate soil temperature
  • Pipe and profile: White conduit, cable trunking, and furniture edge banding

Why TiO2 loading matters

The opacity and whiteness of the final part depend directly on how much TiO2 reaches the polymer matrix. A masterbatch with higher pigment concentration lets you achieve the target opacity at lower addition rates, which saves material cost per part. However, very high TiO2 loadings can create dispersion challenges — agglomerates show up as white specks or streaks in the finished product.

MB-120 is formulated at a concentration that balances these tradeoffs. AGAMA's in-house compounding process uses high-shear twin-screw extrusion to break down TiO2 agglomerates before they reach your hopper.

For applications requiring FDA-compliant white masterbatch (food-contact packaging), contact AGAMA's technical team to confirm the specific TiO2 grade and carrier resin certification for your application.

MB-120 vs. commodity white imports

Imported white masterbatch from Asia is widely available, but there are practical reasons to consider a Mexican source like AGAMA:

  • Lead time: 5-10 business days from Mexico vs. 8-14 weeks from Asia by ocean freight
  • Lot consistency: In-house formulation means AGAMA controls the TiO2 grade, carrier resin, and process parameters lot by lot
  • USMCA tariff advantage: Duty-free entry into the US when rules of origin are met
  • Technical support: Same-timezone, same-language access to the formulator — not a trading company
  • Custom adjustments: Need a slightly higher or lower TiO2 loading? A different carrier resin? AGAMA can adjust the formulation for your specific process

Pairing MB-120 with other AGAMA products

White masterbatch is frequently combined with other concentrates in the same process. Common combinations include:

  • MB-120 + MB-105 Deslizante for white packaging films that need slip and anti-block properties
  • MB-120 + a color masterbatch for pastel shades (the white base provides opacity while the color masterbatch adds the tint)
  • MB-120 + AD-304 UV Protector for outdoor white parts that need weathering resistance
